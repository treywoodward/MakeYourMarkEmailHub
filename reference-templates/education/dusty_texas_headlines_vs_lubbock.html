Lubbock Is Not.

If you have been anywhere near the news this month, you have seen the Texas housing headlines. Sellers slashing prices. Buyers with all the leverage. Texas cities at the top of the list of the most desperate markets in the country. I have had four people ask me about it in the last week alone, so let us talk about it honestly, because both halves of the story matter.

The Part That Is True

The headlines are not wrong. They are just not about us.

Nationally, about 41 percent of homes for sale are going through a price cut. In some Texas metros it is far worse than that. More than half the active listings in San Antonio, Austin and Dallas have taken a cut. Austin has seen the steepest price decline of any major metro in the country since 2022.

There is a clear reason for it. Those cities got a wave of new construction between 2021 and 2023, and statewide, builders now account for close to 30 percent of the homes sitting on the market. When builders need to move standing inventory, they cut, and everyone selling a resale home nearby has to compete with that. That is a supply problem, and it is very real in those markets.

The Part Nobody Reports

Lubbock never had the boom, so it is not having the bust.

We did not get a construction blitz. We did not get the speculative run-up. And the most recent numbers show a market moving in the opposite direction from the headlines. Sales here were up 18 percent over the same month last year. Active listings actually fell. Months of inventory tightened from 4.2 down to 3.7, and anything under six months still favors sellers.

Most telling of all: homes here are closing at 96.6 percent of their original list price. That is not a market where sellers are getting run over. That is a market where a fairly priced home gets a fair offer.

Dusty's Take

“A price cut is not something the market does to you. It is something that happens when you started too high.”

Here is what I want you to take from all of this. Even in Austin, where the headlines are ugliest, homes are still selling every single day. The ones that sell are the ones that were priced against real comps, cleaned up and staged, and marketed with photography that makes somebody stop scrolling. The ones that end up in a price cut statistic are the ones that started with a number based on hope.

That has always been true. A soft market just makes it obvious faster. And in Lubbock, where we have a genuinely healthy market underneath us, doing those three things right is not a defensive move. It is how you end up with multiple offers.

So do not let a Dallas headline talk you out of selling. And do not let a strong local market talk you into being lazy about it either. Both mistakes cost the same thing.

If You Are Buying

There is a real opportunity in this. If you have flexibility on where you land in Texas, the leverage buyers have in those larger metros right now is the most they have had in years. And here at home, our upper price ranges above $500,000 have more than six months of supply, which means room to negotiate that our lower ranges simply do not have. Ask me about it before you write an offer.
