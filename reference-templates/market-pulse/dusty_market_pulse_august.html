What's Moving

Volume is up. Prices are flat. That combination matters.

Lubbock closed 627 homes in June, an 18 percent jump over June of last year, and total dollar volume climbed 28 percent to just over $202 million. At the same time active listings actually fell to 1,700 and months of inventory tightened from 4.2 down to 3.7. For context, a balanced market sits around 6.0, so we are still firmly on the seller side of the line.

What is not moving is price. The median close price came in at $250,000, essentially flat year over year at four tenths of a percent. More buyers are transacting, but they are not bidding prices up. Meanwhile the local job market added 1,800 jobs over the past year, which is the quiet fundamental underneath all of this. People are moving here and buying here.

Dusty's Take

“There is no such thing as the Lubbock market. There are five of them.”

If your home is going to list under $400,000, you are in a seller's market. Under three months of supply means buyers are competing with each other, and a well prepared home priced right is going to get attention fast. Do not let anyone talk you into being timid.

If you are selling above $500,000, it is a different conversation entirely. There is more than six months of supply sitting in that range, and that is a buyer's market by any definition. Your home has to be the best presented option in its price band, not just a good one. That means real staging, professional photography, and a price that acknowledges what your competition looks like. The sellers who understand which market they are actually in are the ones who get their homes sold.

And if you are buying in the upper ranges right now, you have more leverage than you have had in years. Use it.
